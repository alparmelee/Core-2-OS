Part A

MSDS and asset disposal

Documenting hazardous materials and disposing of batteries, toner, and electronics responsibly.

MSDS / SDS documentation

Material Safety Data Sheet / Safety Data Sheet

Required by OSHA, SDS documents contain product composition, health hazard information, first aid guidelines, firefighting measures, and safe disposal steps for hazardous materials like batteries and toner.

Keep SDS sheets accessible wherever hazardous materials are stored or used — technicians should review them before handling unfamiliar chemicals.

Proper battery disposal

Batteries from mobile devices, laptops, and Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS) contain hazardous materials and must never be thrown into regular trash.

Take them to local hazardous waste disposal sites or authorized battery recycling drop-off points.

Proper toner disposal

Toner cartridges should be:

  • Recycled using manufacturer return programs
  • Traded in at local office supply stores for discounts
  • Disposed of according to MSDS guidelines — not standard trash

Spilled toner is a fine particulate — avoid vacuuming with a standard household vacuum; use a toner-rated vacuum or follow SDS cleanup steps.

Proper disposal of devices

Electronic devices and components containing hazardous elements must be taken to authorized local hazardous material or e-waste recycling locations.

Sanitize or destroy storage media before disposal when devices contain sensitive data.

Part B

Climate control, ventilation, and maintenance

Keeping equipment cool, dust-free, and within safe environmental ranges.

Temperature and humidity awareness

Operating equipment creates significant internal heat from CPUs and memory, requiring active cooling to ensure optimal operation.

A relative humidity level of around 50% balances electrostatic discharge (ESD) mitigation with workspace comfort — too dry increases static; too humid risks condensation and corrosion.

Location, equipment placement, and ventilation

Workspaces require adequate ventilation when cleaning dusty computers or handling paper-heavy printer environments.

Position racks and desktops so intake and exhaust airflow are not blocked. Keep vents clear of clutter, papers, and cable bundles.

Dust cleanup — compressed air and vacuums

  • Use a vacuum (preferably one designed for electronics) when cleaning out system dust indoors
  • If using compressed air to blow out heavy dust, move the computer outside the building — blowing dust indoors redistributes contaminants and creates inhalation hazards
Hold fans in place when blowing compressed air so they do not spin freely and generate back-voltage into the motherboard.
Part C

Power management and protection

UPS types for continuous or backup power, and surge suppressor specifications.

Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)

Provides backup power during outages and guards against brownouts (under-voltage) and power surges.

Additional features may include auto-shutdown capabilities, customizable capacities, varied outlet counts, and line noise/power suppression for phone or Ethernet lines.

Standby UPS

Switches from main line power to battery power during outages with a slight transfer delay.

Line-interactive UPS

Dynamically supplements power from the battery as main line voltage drops — handles brownouts without full switchover.

Online UPS

Runs connected systems continuously off battery power while utility power recharges the battery bank. Zero transfer time; highest protection.

Surge suppressor

Absorbs excessive voltage spikes on electrical lines and redirects them safely to the ground.

Key specifications:

  • Joule rating — Measures surge absorption capacity; values over 600 joules offer the highest level of protection
  • Noise filtration — Filters electrical noise based on decibel and frequency ratings; higher decibel ratings provide better filtration
  • Surge amp rating — Higher amp ratings indicate superior suppressor performance and quality
  • UL 1449 voltage let-through rating — Maximum voltage allowed through to connected devices; lower ratings (e.g., 330V vs. 500V) indicate higher quality protection
