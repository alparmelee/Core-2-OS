Safety and Environmental Controls

Part A

ESD prevention and component handling

Protect sensitive electronics from electrostatic discharge during service, transport, and storage.

ESD straps

Wristband devices that equalize electrical potential between the technician and the chassis, featuring breakaway snaps for mobility.

Never attach an ESD strap directly to a building’s electrical ground. Connect to the equipment chassis or a proper ESD common ground point on the bench.

ESD mats and pads

Conductive surface mats or desk pads used to safely balance static potential when working on components or standing near equipment.

Mats should be grounded through a current-limiting resistor and kept clean — dust and insulators on the surface reduce effectiveness.

Antistatic bags

Specially designed metallic or static-shielding bags that protect isolated computer parts from static discharges during transport or storage.

Distinguish antistatic (pink) bags from static-shielding (metallic) bags — only shielding bags provide full Faraday-cage protection for bare boards.

Proper component handling and storage

Always handle circuit boards by their outer edges rather than exposed circuits or chips.

Store components in climate-controlled environments (50–80°F / 10–27°C with low humidity), using silica gel packs or original packaging with bubble wrap to protect against moisture and physical damage.

Part B

Electrical and workspace safety

Prevent shock, residual voltage injuries, and trip hazards in the work area.

Disconnect power

Unplug power cords from computers, printers, or peripheral devices before opening cases to prevent electrical shock.

Power off, unplug, and press the power button briefly to discharge standby power on some systems before working inside the chassis.

High-voltage components and grounding

Never open or service power supplies, CRT displays, or laser printers — internal capacitors can retain dangerous residual voltage even when unplugged.

Ensure equipment chassis and data center racks are properly connected to electrical grounds to redirect fault current away from technicians.

Never remove ground prongs from power plugs. A missing ground defeats the safety path for fault current.

Cable management

Keep walkways clear by bundling cables using Velcro or cable ties, and taping or covering any cables crossing floor paths to eliminate trip hazards.

Label runs, maintain slack for airflow in racks, and route power and data separately where possible to reduce interference.

Part C

Personal safety and environmental compliance

Protect yourself, handle equipment safely, and follow regulations for disposal and workplace hazards.

Personal protective equipment (PPE)

  • Safety goggles — When handling hazardous battery chemicals or working inside printers
  • Air filter mask — To avoid inhaling fine airborne dust or printer toner during maintenance

Select PPE matched to the specific hazard — toner, battery acid, and metal shavings each require different protection.

Lifting techniques

Lift heavy computers or equipment using leg strength rather than bending the back — keep the load close, feet shoulder-width apart, and avoid twisting while carrying.

Use specialized lift equipment (server lifts, pallet jacks, two-person carries) for data center rack installation and heavy UPS units.

Fire safety

Unplug burning equipment immediately if safe to do so.

Use dry chemical, carbon dioxide (CO₂), or FM-200 suppression systems for electronic equipment — not water or foam, which can conduct electricity and cause further damage or shock.

  • Class C (electrical)
  • CO₂ extinguisher
  • FM-200 data center

Compliance with government regulations

Adhere to local building codes for electrical and fire safety, and observe environmental regulations regarding proper disposal and recycling of hazardous e-waste — such as old batteries, CRTs, and computers.

Many jurisdictions require certified e-waste recyclers; never dispose of toner, batteries, or mercury-containing devices in standard trash.
